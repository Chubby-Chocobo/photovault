PhotoVault

Privacy Policy

Effective 12 November 2026

PhotoVault is a macOS app that backs up the Photos library on your Mac to storage you choose: a folder in your own Google Drive, and local or network (NAS) folders. This policy explains what data the app handles and where it goes.

It covers the PhotoVault app distributed on the Mac App Store. A copy you build yourself from the source code uses your own Google and Dropbox projects, with the permissions you set up there, and is not covered by this policy. A build from source can also include the browser import from Google Drive, which asks for the read-only drive.readonly permission; the App Store version does not.

In short: PhotoVault has no server. The developer does not receive, store or see your photos, your Google data or anything else about you.

Data the app handles

  • Your Photos library. With your permission, the app reads photos, videos and their metadata (date, camera, location, albums) from the Photos library on your Mac. It never changes or deletes anything in the library.
  • Backup copies. The app copies those files, plus small metadata files ("sidecars" with dates, camera, location and album names), to the destinations you set up. Nothing is sent anywhere else.
  • Imported folders. If you choose a folder on this Mac to import, the app reads the photos and videos in it on this Mac only, and never changes or deletes anything in the folder. Each imported file is backed up like a photo from the Photos library, and its sidecar at your destination records the name of the folder you picked and the file's path inside it. Nothing else about the folder leaves your Mac.
  • Cloud folders you import. If you import from Dropbox, the app reads only the folder you choose in Dropbox. If you import from Google Drive, the app reads the Google Drive for desktop folder you pick on this Mac, like any other folder on the Mac, and no Google permission is involved. It never changes or deletes anything there. Each imported file is backed up like a photo from the Photos library, and its sidecar at your destination records the name of the folder you picked and the file's path inside it.
  • Local app data. A database of what has been backed up, settings and logs stay on your Mac, in the app's own folders. Logs do not contain file names or locations.

Google user data

If you connect Google Drive for backups, the App Store version of PhotoVault requests only these permissions:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file: to create a backup folder in your Drive, upload your photos into it, and read back the files it created to verify and resume backups. This permission gives no access to any file in your Drive that PhotoVault did not create.
  • openid and email: to show which Google account is connected.

The App Store version does not request drive.readonly or any other Google permission, and it does not read other files in your Drive. To import photos that are already in Google Drive, use the Google Drive for desktop folder on your Mac (see above).

Your Google data is used only to provide the backup feature you asked for. It is transferred only between your Mac and Google. It is not sent to the developer or to any third party, not used for advertising, not sold, and not used to train AI or machine learning models. No person reads it.

The sign-in tokens are stored only in the macOS Keychain on your Mac. Signing out of Google in the app revokes the sign-in with Google. Start over in the app deletes the tokens from this Mac; deleting a sign-in from this Mac does not remove the permission at Google. You can remove PhotoVault's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Files already in your Drive remain yours; you can keep or delete them.

PhotoVault's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Dropbox data

If you import from Dropbox, PhotoVault asks only for read-only access: files.metadata.read and files.content.read, to list and download the files in the folder you choose, and account_info.read, to show which Dropbox account is connected. It cannot change or delete anything in Dropbox.

Your Dropbox data is used only for the import you asked for. It is transferred only between your Mac and Dropbox. It is not sent to the developer or to any third party, not used for advertising, not sold, and not used to train AI or machine learning models. The Dropbox access token is stored only in the macOS Keychain on your Mac. You can remove PhotoVault's access at any time in the Connected apps settings of your Dropbox account. Signing out of Dropbox in the app only removes the folder browser's sign-in. Each Dropbox import's sign-in is deleted from this Mac once the import is finished or cancelled and no file of it is still waiting to upload. The permission stays in your Dropbox account until you remove PhotoVault there, and Start over deletes every token from this Mac.

Place names

To name folders after places, the app can ask Apple's geocoding service (built into macOS) for the name of an area. It sends only an approximate point, rounded to about 1 km, not your photo's exact location. You can turn place names off in Settings. Apple's handling of these requests is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting services.
  • No accounts with the developer, and no developer server.
  • Does not read files outside the Photos library and the folders on your Mac and the Dropbox folders you choose.

Security

Connections to Google and Dropbox use HTTPS. Backup files are stored as normal files: Google encrypts data at rest in Drive, and the security of local and NAS folders is up to you. PhotoVault does not add its own encryption.

Deleting your data

  • Signing out of Google in the app stops the backup account's access. You can also remove PhotoVault at myaccount.google.com/permissions. To stop Dropbox access, remove PhotoVault in your Dropbox account. Start over in the app deletes every sign-in from this Mac.
  • Delete the backup folder in Google Drive or on your disks to remove the copies.
  • To uninstall the App Store version, move PhotoVault to the Trash (or delete it from Launchpad). Removing the app's folder in ~/Library/Containers/com.annh.photovault removes the local data, but not the sign-in tokens in the macOS Keychain. Use Start over in the app before you uninstall it, or search for com.annh.photovault in Keychain Access and delete the items it finds. To stop access at the provider as well, remove PhotoVault in your Google or Dropbox account as described above.

Children

PhotoVault is not directed to children under 13.
